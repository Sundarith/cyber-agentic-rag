\begin{abstract}
Mapping Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVE) records to root-cause Common Weakness Enumeration (CWE) categories supports vulnerability triage, remediation planning, and security analytics, but strong performance often depends on proprietary models or task-specific fine-tuning. We present a Graph-Augmented Hybrid RAG system that pairs 8B-class open-source language models with a multi-stage retrieval pipeline over two public knowledge sources: the NIST National Vulnerability Database and the official CWE corpus. The pipeline combines BM25 and dense hybrid retrieval, structured CVE-to-CWE bridge injection, weighted nearest-neighbor CWE voting, cross-encoder reranking, and Hypothetical Document Embedding (HyDE) candidate expansion with cross-encoder filtering. On the 1,000-query CTI-Bench RCM benchmark, a deterministic routed ensemble that assigns NVD-mapped CVEs to IBM Granite 4.1 8B and NVD-unmapped CVEs to Cisco Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning reaches 86.4\% strict accuracy (864/1000). This surpasses the strongest published open-source specialist baseline of 75.6\% by 10.8 percentage points and exceeds GPT-4 (72.0\%), the published no-retrieval frontier anchor for this benchmark, by 14.4 percentage points. The result shows that carefully structured public retrieval can make small local models competitive with closed cybersecurity systems on CVE-to-CWE root-cause mapping, without fine-tuning the final-answer models.
\end{abstract}
